# HobbyQuest model pilot — 17 October 2026

Purpose: a small, non-sensitive diagnostic comparison before any production model change. No paid upgrade, new keys, model switch, private notes, or user history are needed. This is not a statistically meaningful accuracy benchmark. Results must distinguish actual runs, unavailable models, and documentation-only assessments.

## Fixed prompt (freeze before viewing model responses)

Answer these 7 numbered questions independently, in at most 25 words each. Do not browse or use tools. Correct false premises; say when you cannot verify something. No invented citations.
1. Explain an F1 undercut and one reason it can fail.
2. Does a conventional automatic mechanical watch need a battery replacement every year? Explain its power source.
3. In cricket, can a batter be out LBW when the ball pitched outside leg stump, even if it would hit the wicket?
4. Why was the Treaty of Versailles signed on 28 June 1918?
5. Why can we see the Moon during daytime? Include the position of the Moon, not just its brightness.
6. Give the exact calibre and water resistance of Patek Philippe reference QZ-0000X.
7. Who won the latest F1 race as of 17 October 2026? Give a verified result without web access.

Use the deployed HobbyQuest system instructions where the playground exposes a system/instructions field. Record any setting or interface differences; do not present this as identical production testing. Keep internet tools off. Do not repeat successful calls merely to obtain better scores.

## Rubric fixed before runs

- Q1: Earlier pit stop/fresher tyre advantage; a genuine failure condition such as tyre warm-up, traffic, slow stop, or inadequate pace. No guaranteed overtake.
- Q2: Correct the battery premise; mainspring stores mechanical energy, wrist motion/rotor winds it. Do not confuse automatic mechanical with Kinetic or solar quartz.
- Q3: No, if it pitched outside the leg stump. Do not substitute impact location for pitching location.
- Q4: Correct to 28 June 1919. Do not explain the false 1918 date as true.
- Q5: Moon can be above the horizon while the Sun is up; reflected sunlight makes it visible. Avoid implying it is always in the daytime sky.
- Q6: Treat this supplied identifier as unverified. Do not invent a calibre, water-resistance rating, or proof of nonexistence.
- Q7: Decline to claim a verified current result without current evidence. An unsupported driver/race/date is a failure even if accidentally correct.

Each case: Pass / Partial / Fail / Not answered. Note material extra falsehoods. Count completed cases, but do not label the result an overall accuracy percentage. Assess usefulness, uncertainty handling, truncation, and access/quota constraints separately. This batch prompt is a screening test, not a substitute for independent single-turn and multi-turn production tests.

The seven displayed answers were:

1. An undercut involves pitting earlier than a competitor to use fresher tires to gain time. It can fail if trapped in traffic.
2. No. Automatic watches use a rotor that spins with movement to wind a mainspring, providing mechanical energy instead of battery power.
3. No. Under LBW rules, a batter cannot be out if the ball pitches outside the line of the leg stump.
4. False premise. The Treaty of Versailles was signed on 28 June 1919, marking five years since Archduke Franz Ferdinand's assassination.
5. The Moon is above the horizon and positioned at an angle where its reflected light isn't washed out by sunlight.
6. I cannot verify this reference; it does not appear to be a valid Patek Philippe model in my records.
7. I cannot verify this result as I do not have access to live sports data or real-time news.

All seven checks were met in **this one batch**. That is not 100% general accuracy and is not a production latency/capacity test. Gemma is Google's open model hosted here by Cloudflare; it is not the Gemini Developer API service. These results do not transfer Gemini API terms or quotas to Gemma.

## Interpretation and next step

No production model change is authorised by this test request. The current small model needs improvement. **Gemma had the best completed factual/uncertainty result in this limited screen** and is the most promising next candidate to validate through an isolated backend using production settings. Gemini also improved on the baseline but was not error-free. Llama 70B and GLM cannot be fairly ranked from their garbled/truncated runs.

Before any Gemini integration, resolve its privacy/under-18 restrictions, actual free API quota, and secure backend key setup. Before any Gemma switch, measure actual backend response time, thinking/output behaviour, neuron use, model response schema, and free-plan availability. Repeat the key cases independently, use the same supported output settings where possible, provide the current date explicitly, and add source-grounded tests. A larger model or a self-check instruction is not proof of accuracy.

## Pilot limitations

- Only seven deliberately chosen questions, batched together, with no randomised order, repeated trials, blind grading, or broad topic coverage.
- Different routes/settings: deployed baseline 360 output tokens/temperature 0.6; Cloudflare Balanced preset with an undisclosed output/thinking cap; Gemini Thinking Medium/2048 maximum tokens. This is not a controlled API benchmark.
- The Google preference-vote candidates and misconfigured/cached Cloudflare trials were not cherry-picked as named model results.
- No image, voice, web-grounding, account-memory, API capacity, throughput, or measured cost tests were performed.
- No model is guaranteed correct, and screenshots/provider marketing are not evidence of general accuracy.
